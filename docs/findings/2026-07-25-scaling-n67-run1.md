### T1/T2: both models fail, and they fail differently

M1 over-predicts at every N (all z₁ negative). M2 over-predicts at N=4,5,6 and
then **under**-predicts at N=7 — its z crosses zero between N=6 and N=7. A
one-parameter retention law fitted at N=3 cannot bend that way, so neither model
describes this device across the full range.

Note that M1's z at N=7 is −1.79, i.e. it would *individually* pass at 1.96σ.
The registered test requires both N=6 and N=7, so T1 fails on N=6. Reporting
the N=7 pass without the N=6 failure would be exactly the kind of selective
reading the registration exists to prevent.

### T3: the model race split, contradicting the existing ranking

M2 is closer at N=4, 5 and 6; M1 is closer at N=7. The registered question was
whether M2's lead in `repeat-judgments.json` extrapolates to the largest N. **It
does not.** The registration named this outcome in advance: *"if it loses, the
existing ranking does not extrapolate and the paper must say so."* The paper
must say so.

### T4: monotone decay confirmed

0.9915 > 0.7313 > 0.5840 > 0.4814 > 0.4205. The chain is uniform enough that the
curve is not being driven by qubit selection.

## The N=7 anomaly (the interesting part)

**Retention is not monotone in N**: 99.15%, 97.51%, 97.33%, **96.29%**,
**98.13%**. N=7 retains *more* of its ideal advantage than N=4, 5 or 6, despite
having the most gates (22 cz) and the lowest fidelity (P(0…0) = 0.7839).

Two independent signs that N=7 is genuinely out of family, not a fluctuation:

1. Its fidelity drop from N=6 is small (0.8008 → 0.7839) relative to N=5 → N=6
   (0.8691 → 0.8008), even though cz rose by the same 4.
2. **Its ZNE extrapolation overshoots the ideal**: 0.4437 against an ideal of
   0.4286. No physical noise model produces more advantage than the noiseless
   circuit, so the linear extrapolation is being driven past its range by the
   fold points.

This is what breaks both models: they assume retention degrades smoothly in gate
count, and at N=7 it does not. Whether the cause is the payoff geometry at odd N,
the particular qubits at the end of the chain (98), or an extrapolation artefact
is **not determined by this run**. It needs the Task 9 repeats on other
calibration days before it is called a result rather than an observation.

| 7 | 0.0075 | **0.0151** | **not improved — overshoots** |

The pre-submission gate (strict majority) was met at 3/5, and it was met for the
right reason: the gate is about whether the batch is worth quota, not a
prediction that every cell improves.

## What this changes

- **N=6,7 hardware data now exists** (item 4), on a chain that provably matches
  its registration.
- **The cz-exponential model's lead does not extrapolate.** Any paper text
  ranking the five models must be scoped to N≤5, or restated as "leads at N≤5,
  splits at N=7".
- **Both registered models failing is the honest headline**, and it is the
  second registered failure reported straight today (T4a/T4b in the topology
  batch was the first). The moat is the reporting, not the pass rate.
- The N=7 retention anomaly is the most interesting open thread and is the
  reason to run Task 9's repeats.
