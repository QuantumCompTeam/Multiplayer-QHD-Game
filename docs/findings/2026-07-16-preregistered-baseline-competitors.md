## The three competitors (conditional protocol, same as primary)

Each anchors on the day's N=3 mitigated fold-1 advantage A3 and predicts
that day's N=4/5 mitigated fold-1 advantages:

1. **cz-exponential** — advantage retention decays exponentially in the
   run's transpiled fold-1 cz count: r = (A3/1.0)^(1/cz3),
   pred_N = ideal_N · r^(cz_N). cz counts from that run's `pub_meta`
   (run 1: 6, 9, 14).
2. **device-model (anchored)** — the run's own stored
   `predictions.device_model.<N>.noro.advantage` (the pipeline's
   backend-calibration prediction; `noro` is the mitigated-fold-1
   comparator), rescaled by A3/dm_3.
3. **constant-retention (no-decay null)** — N=3's fractional deficit applied
   unchanged: pred_N = ideal_N · (A3/1.0).

Judged with the **primary registration's σ_predictive** as a shared
yardstick (model-comparison scores, not per-competitor calibrated
intervals). Registered score: z4² + z5² per run, cumulative across repeats,
lower is better; the primary p_eff model competes on the same score.

## What would settle it

- p_eff wins: cumulative score below all competitors across repeats — the
  one-parameter depolarizing model earns its place in the paper.
- cz-exponential wins: the paper's model section should lead with per-gate
  decay and demote p_eff to a physical interpretation of r.
- device-model wins: calibration data explains the scaling; both effective
  models are redundant.
- constant-retention wins: N=3 tells you everything; the scaling adds no
  information beyond the anchor (would also mean the registered N=5 deficit
  did not reproduce).
