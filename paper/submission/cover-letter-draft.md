Dear Editors,

Please consider “Incentive Compatibility and Payoff Robustness in Multiplayer
Quantum Games: Theory and Hardware Evidence” by Prithvi Raghu and Aasa Singh
Bhui as a regular article for IEEE Transactions on Quantum Engineering.

The manuscript studies an implementation problem in multiplayer quantum
games: cooperative output statistics can remain robust while individual
incentives fail. We derive an exact restricted-strategy phase boundary,
construct an alternative cooperative branch for arbitrary player count,
and give an explicit unrestricted counterstrategy. A compact classical
representation enables exact ideal GHZ payoff evaluation through 128
players, separating analytical scalability from the seven-player physical
demonstration. A payoff sensitivity analysis and compilation controls limit
the interpretation of high welfare retention and apparent topology effects.

The experimental record includes committed predictions, failed noise-model
extrapolations, and newly registered tests of every player's Hawk and Dove
deviations through seven players on IBM hardware. The manuscript reports
statistical limitations and failed acceptance criteria alongside positive
results. Its contribution concerns quantum-software validation, incentive
tests, and observable sensitivity; it does not claim computational quantum
advantage or a deployed economic mechanism.

The accompanying source archive and repository evidence map support review
of the proofs, code, counts, and registration provenance.

Sincerely,
Prithvi Raghu and Aasa Singh Bhui
Vellore Institute of Technology, Vellore, India
